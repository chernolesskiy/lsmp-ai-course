Neuron
Adds up its inputs times their weights, plus a bias, then decides.
Weight
How much one input counts. Negative means it counts against.
Bias
A number added to the total. It makes the neuron fire more or less easily.
Fires (activation)
The neuron says 1 (yes) when its total is above 0.
Layer
A group of neurons side by side. One layer feeds the next.

Check your understanding

What does a weight do in a neuron?

  1. It switches the neuron on or off when an input changes
  2. It sets how much one input counts towards the total
  3. It stores the answer the neuron gave last time
  4. It counts how many inputs the neuron has

Each input is multiplied by its weight. A big weight means that input matters a lot; a negative weight counts against firing.

What does the bias do?

  1. It multiplies every input by the same amount to make it bigger
  2. It decides which input is the most important one
  3. It makes the neuron ignore any input that is 0
  4. It moves the total up or down, so the neuron fires more or less easily

The bias is added to the total. A very negative bias means the inputs have to work hard to get the total above 0.

Why can't a single neuron learn XOR?

  1. No single straight line puts both 1s on one side and both 0s on the other
  2. XOR needs more than two inputs, and this neuron only has two
  3. The neuron didn't train for enough rounds to find the line
  4. The learning rate was too small to ever reach the answer

The 1s sit in opposite corners, so any straight line leaves at least one point on the wrong side. The search tried 729 settings and found none.

When a neuron or network trains, what changes?

  1. The training data
  2. The inputs it is given
  3. Its weights and biases
  4. The rule "fire if the total is above 0"

Training is guess, check, nudge: after each mistake the weights and biases are nudged a little. The data and the firing rule stay the same.
