Error landscapes

Every line is one pair of numbers: a slope m and an intercept c. Give each line its error score as a height, and all the lines together make a landscape. The best line is the lowest point.

Every line we tried

The idea: a bowl

A made-up landscape that shows the idea cleanly: one lowest point, and contour rings around it. On the map, the closer the rings, the steeper the ground. Every ring is a set of lines that are all equally good.

3

Local and global minima

Real error landscapes can have more than one dip. The global minimum is the lowest point of all. A local minimum is lower than everything right around it, but not the lowest. Searching every point always finds the global minimum. Walking downhill (Lesson 2) can get stuck in a local one.

4

More than two numbers

With a third number we run out of directions to draw the height in. But the map still works: every point in this box is one model (three numbers), and the shells join models with the same error score, like the rings on the map, now nested like the layers of an onion. The best model sits in the middle. Real AIs have billions of numbers. Nobody can picture that, but the job is the same: find the lowest point.
